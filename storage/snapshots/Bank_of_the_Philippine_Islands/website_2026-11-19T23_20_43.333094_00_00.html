Insider Spotlight

  • BPI earns three Philippine awards for banking innovation
  • Digital account opening requires just one ID and a selfie
  • Financial literacy initiative reaches over 10,000 participants



Bank of the Philippine Islands (BPI) is making everyday banking more accessible to Filipinos by simplifying account opening, improving digital services, and expanding financial education, as banks increasingly compete on convenience and customer experience.

The bank's efforts earned three Philippine country-level recognitions at the inaugural ASEAN Banking Awards 2026 in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, where it received the Most Inclusive Bank, Best App Experience, and Best Onboarding Experience awards.

Simpler banking, wider access

BPI's digital onboarding process allows customers to open accounts using one government-issued ID and a selfie. National ID holders can also verify their identity through a selfie alone, eliminating the need to upload a physical ID.

The streamlined process reduces documentation requirements and makes account opening more convenient for Filipinos seeking access to formal banking services.

BPI's initiatives were detailed in a company release, which also highlighted its efforts to improve mobile banking through features such as BEA Chat, Mobile Key, and Virtual Investment Advisor (VIA).

Financial inclusion beyond digital

The bank's Most Inclusive Bank recognition also acknowledged RealiTalk, its financial literacy program that has reached more than 10,000 participants, according to award organizer Twimbit.

The initiative complements BPI's customer support services and traditional banking channels, extending financial education alongside its digital offerings.

“These awards affirm our commitment to helping Filipinos do more, achieve more, and grow more through accessible and innovative financial solutions. By making banking simpler and more seamless, we empower our customers to confidently pursue opportunities, build their dreams, and create a better future for themselves and their families,” said BPI president and CEO TG Limcaoco, 

Why it matters

For Filipinos, easier account opening and more accessible financial tools can help reduce barriers to saving, managing money, and accessing banking services.

Organized by research and advisory firm Twimbit, the ASEAN Banking Awards assess banks across Southeast Asia using more than 60 parameters covering digital, service, brand, and employee experience.

The recognitions underscore how customer convenience, financial literacy, and digital accessibility are becoming important measures of banking competitiveness. —Princess Daisy C. Ominga | Ed: Corrie S. Narisma
